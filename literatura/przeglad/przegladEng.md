In web application development, choosing the right technology is one of the key architectural decisions that directly impact system performance, scalability, and maintenance costs. Among the available solutions, two frameworks stand out: Laravel, based on PHP, and NestJS, based on Node.js. Both represent fundamentally different approaches to architecture and execution.
PHP, the foundation of Laravel, operates on a synchronous model using PHP-FPM processes, where each HTTP request is handled by a separate process or thread. In contrast, Node.js, on which NestJS is built, employs an event-driven model with non-blocking I/O, allowing for the simultaneous handling of multiple connections within a single thread. This fundamental difference suggests that both technologies may exhibit significantly different performance characteristics depending on the specific nature of the load.


Research Objective
The objective of this study is to conduct an experimental comparative analysis of the performance of web applications implemented using Laravel and NestJS. As part of this research, functionally equivalent applications will be developed in both technologies and subjected to performance tests under various load scenarios.

Specific objectives include:
• Comparing application response times under varying loads,
• Analyzing system resource consumption (CPU, memory),
• Evaluating system throughput (requests per second),
• Analyzing the impact of the data-access layer on application performance.

Hypotheses / Research Theses

H1: Under low load, the response-time performance of Laravel and NestJS applications is comparable for simple data-access operations.

H2: Under high concurrency, NestJS scales better than Laravel: its response time grows more slowly with increasing load.

H3: The greater the relational complexity of a data-access operation, the larger the difference in response time between Laravel and NestJS relative to a simple single-table operation.

The hypotheses state the expected relationships at a general level. Their
operational definitions, including endpoints, concurrency levels, metrics,
thresholds, repetitions, confidence intervals and decision rules, are specified
in the methodology rather than in this literature review.

2. Literature Review
2.1. Overview of Laravel and the PHP Ecosystem
Laravel is a mature PHP framework with a rich ecosystem, advanced caching, routing, and middleware, making it a preferred choice for complex enterprise applications [2, 3]. The trade-off for this extensibility is higher resource overhead compared to lighter PHP frameworks. Ahmed et al. [3] demonstrated CodeIgniter's advantage over Laravel in all load scenarios, confirming that Laravel's overhead is most evident during intensive operations—lighter frameworks consistently achieve shorter response times at the cost of a reduced feature set [3, 4]. Simultaneously, Abutaleb et al. [5] showed that Laravel achieves surprisingly good throughput in simple scenarios, while its weaknesses appear during complex operations, confirming that its performance profile is strongly dependent on the workload. Importantly, the use of optimization techniques (e.g., route caching, eager loading) yields significant performance gains, highlighting the framework's high potential for tuning [3]. From the perspective of database systems, Rahman et al. [6] showed that the choice of RDBMS can sometimes have a greater impact on performance than the framework itself, suggesting that proper database selection can effectively mitigate framework-specific differences. Regarding deployment, Dhaniswara et al. [7] demonstrated that FrankenPHP with Laravel Octane offers better responsiveness and higher throughput than traditional PHP-FPM, albeit at the cost of significantly higher memory consumption. Crucially, for relational data collections, neither server architecture yielded improvement—the bottleneck remained the ORM and JSON serialization [7].

2.2. Characteristics of NestJS and the Node.js Ecosystem
Node.js, the foundation of NestJS, implements an event-driven model with non-blocking I/O, allowing a single thread to handle multiple connections simultaneously, thereby eliminating costly context switching [8]. This architecture is widely considered to provide better scalability and resource utilization than classic multithreaded servers, particularly for the C10k problem [8]. Lundar and Grønli [1] confirmed this empirically, showing that an event-driven server (Node.js) achieves significantly lower response times and CPU usage than a multithreaded server (Jetty), and that using WebSockets radically reduces network traffic compared to long-polling. Krishna [9] demonstrated the superiority of Node.js (Express) over Django and Spring Boot in terms of latency, throughput, and memory usage. Production data supports this; PayPal reported significant improvements after migrating from Java to Node.js [9]. While Node.js remains limited in CPU-intensive tasks, worker threads and clustering mitigate this issue [9]. NestJS introduces an opinionated, modular architecture with built-in dependency injection and native TypeScript support, making it optimal for large systems requiring long-term maintenance [11]. Kuffel and Walter [10] benchmarked seven Node.js frameworks, including Express, Fastify, NestJS on the Express adapter and NestJS on the Fastify adapter, across five synthetic workloads: static JSON, CPU-bound computation, Redis I/O, input validation and error handling, each repeated ten times. Fastify was the fastest Node.js framework in most scenarios, while Express, NestJS and Feathers formed the slowest group; the additional NestJS layer over Express reduced throughput further, whereas NestJS with the Fastify adapter stayed close to Fastify itself. All tested Node.js applications lowered median latency during the first seconds under constant load, which the authors attribute to V8 optimizations, an effect absent from the Go and Python reference implementations. Because the benchmark isolates individual framework features rather than full applications, it characterizes overhead but does not represent a production workload. Alasmar [12] benchmarked Express, Fastify and NestJS on AWS Lambda and found that NestJS had the longest cold start and the highest memory footprint, but the lowest latency and error rate at medium load and higher throughput at high load, although all three frameworks showed high failure rates attributable to platform limits. Since the study was conducted in a serverless setting, its results indicate that NestJS overhead is concentrated at initialization rather than per request, but they cannot be directly transferred to long-running containerized deployments. Arroziqi and Susanty [13] showed that the modular monolith in NestJS improves maintainability and stability, although traditional monoliths may outperform it in read-intensive scenarios. Pratama and Raharja [14] confirmed that containerized environments are the optimal platform for Node.js applications.

2.3. Object-Relational Mapping (ORM) and the Data Layer
The ORM layer has a critical impact on performance. Halimi [15] demonstrated that Eloquent in Laravel exhibits significantly higher overhead for complex queries with multiple joins, being approximately 10× slower than raw SQL for queries involving four joins. In the Node.js ecosystem, Zhadko-Bazilevych [16] compared Sequelize, Prisma, and TypeORM, showing that no single ORM dominates all scenarios: Sequelize excels in simple queries, Prisma scales better under parallel load, and TypeORM offers comprehensive support for hierarchical structures. These studies indicate that the choice of ORM and SQL generation strategy can impact performance as much as the choice of the framework itself.

2.4. Existing Comparative Studies of Web Frameworks
Comparative studies indicate that Node.js typically achieves higher performance in I/O-intensive scenarios, while PHP remains competitive in database operations and synchronous tasks [18, 19, 20]. Lei et al. [18] and Odeniran et al. [19] showed a consistent advantage for Node.js in throughput and latency, which increases with data scale. An exception is the study by Siahaan and Wijaya [17], where Laravel achieved an average response time more than 6 times shorter than that of Express.js when performing large MySQL read queries. This suggests that the synchronous PHP-FPM model can be more efficient than a single-threaded event loop in read-heavy operations. In a direct comparison of five REST API frameworks on Docker, Szewczyk and Skublewska-Paszkowska [21] found Laravel to be the slowest while using the most CPU, whereas Express.js occupied an intermediate position. Zima and Barszcz [11] confirm that ExpressJS is faster under low traffic, while NestJS offers a superior architecture for complex projects.

2.5. Performance Testing Methodology
The reliability of comparative studies depends on proper tool selection. Khlamov et al. [22] showed that JMeter outperforms Postman under high traffic volumes, offering better scalability and control. Barford and Crovella [23] demonstrated that traditional benchmarks often underestimate real-world server requirements compared to models that mirror actual HTTP traffic statistics. A review by Yenuguli et al. [24] confirms that JMeter is recommended for simulating high production loads due to its mature ecosystem and comprehensive monitoring capabilities. Tofan [25] identified two recurring methodological weaknesses in prior Docker-based performance comparisons: the absence of identical, explicitly declared CPU and memory limits, and the reporting of average response times rather than tail-latency percentiles. Addressing these gaps, he equalized container resource caps and measured p90 and p95 latency across twenty repetitions per scenario. Under such controlled conditions, a monolithic Node.js/Express implementation outperformed a two-service HTTP/REST decomposition in latency and throughput, while consuming over three times less memory. Throughput saturation in both architectures pointed to the database layer rather than the application as the primary bottleneck. The methodological principles adopted in that study — equalized resource limits, percentile-based metrics, and repeated measurements — are applied in the present research as well.

A review of available literature reveals several significant research gaps. Previous comparative studies have mainly focused on comparing Laravel with Express.js or other minimalist REST API frameworks. However, few have directly compared Laravel with NestJS—a framework which, unlike Express.js, features architectural complexity and a wealth of features comparable to Laravel. Furthermore, most studies are conducted in heterogeneous environments, making it difficult to generalize results. This paper aims to fill these gaps by directly comparing Laravel and NestJS in a controlled Docker container environment.
